\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What changed the conclusions, and what did not.}
On this family, none of the conventions we tested changed which method was
cheapest. The accuracy target, the instance regime and the problem dimension
did change the order below it. For SBB-Dual against AGD-SDAJ-BH, the changes
of lower-place ordering with the
accuracy target and with rank that we found at $n=100$ recur at $n=500$ only
above $r=50$ (\cref{sec:n500,sec:highrank}); the pair that does not involve
SBB-Dual, Dykstra-APM against AGD-SDAJ-BH, does not recur by $r=150$ at
$n=500$. On matrices built from equity returns the degree of invalidity
reverses the order as well, both for SBB-Dual and for Anderson-APM against
AGD-SDAJ-BH (\cref{sec:thesis}). The stopping rule changed the achieved
accuracy by a factor of 383. The accounting rule (accepted-only against
all-trial) changed cost ratios but no ordering. The cost unit (\EVDs\ against
wall-clock time) changed cost ratios and, in half of the ten timing sessions, the
ordering below first place too: repeating the matched-tolerance timing
measurement five times per dimension
(\cref{sec:evd-unit}) shows the single cheapest method is unaffected in
every session, but which method is dearest overall is not, at $n=100$. Two
line-search details changed whether methods converged at all. The summary
that the evidence supports is conservative: the protocol matters for the
\emph{magnitude} of reported advantages, for the ordering of methods that
are close in cost, and, for the cost unit specifically, sometimes for which
method is worst; it did not matter for identifying a clear first place.
Where methods are within a factor of about two, conclusions drawn under a
single convention or a single timing session should be treated with
caution.

\paragraph{Why an exact family.}
The regime dependence in \cref{sec:cells} is organized mainly by rank, one of
the three quantities the construction controls. Finding it requires both an
exact $\Xs$, so that forward error is not contaminated by an approximate
reference, and independent control of the spectral structure at the
solution. Real matrices supply neither. The same machinery then showed the
dimension dependence of that finding: at $n=500$ the same rank effect, for
SBB-Dual against AGD-SDAJ-BH, changes the order only between $r=50$ and
$r=75$ (\cref{sec:highrank}), so a grid chosen at one dimension can miss the
crossover at another.

\needspace{5\baselineskip}
\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Problem size.} Results are reported at $n=100$ and $n=500$
    (270 instances each), with 135 more at $n=500$ and $r\in\{75,100,150\}$
    for every variant except unmodified AGD-SDAJ. The crossover between
    SBB-Dual and AGD-SDAJ-BH
    lies between $r=5$ and $r=20$ at $n=100$ and between $r=50$ and $r=75$
    at $n=500$; on data-seeded instances at $n=550$ SBB-Dual is still slightly
    behind at $r=100$, and at $n=2105$ (\cref{sec:us2105}) the gap closes to
    within one \EVD\ by $r=400$ without a resolved crossover. For this pair
    the crossover is therefore bracketed at $n=100$ and $n=500$ and only
    bounded below at the two larger dimensions, where it lies higher; the
    data do not support a functional form, and the largest panel contributes
    four instances per rank with weak replication. Without SBB-Dual, the
    exchange of Dykstra-APM and AGD-SDAJ-BH seen at $n=100$ has not occurred
    by $r=150$ at $n=500$, and at $n=2105$ the order
    of Anderson-APM and AGD-SDAJ-BH is not monotone in rank.
    Dykstra-APM is censored at $n=500$ by its iteration cap.
  \item \emph{Exact recovery of the instances.} The three synthetic sets
    (675 instances) are archived in full (\cref{app:artifact}), so a reader
    can use the exact instances of this paper. Regenerating them instead is
    reliable only on a LAPACK build that returns the degenerate kernel basis
    in the same form: another build may return it rotated, which yields
    different members of the same family; the recorded fingerprints detect
    this but cannot undo it.
    The equity-derived sets (\cref{sec:thesis,sec:us2105}) are a stricter
    case: built from vendor price data that may not be redistributed, they
    cannot be regenerated by a reader at all, from fingerprints or otherwise,
    and two of them (the perturbed $n=2105$ panel and the bulk-level pilot of
    \cref{sec:highrank}) have no manifest or fingerprint recorded even for
    that limited check.
  \item \emph{Synthetic family.} The KKT family is synthetic. Its $G$
    matrices are correlation-like but are not estimated from data, and all of
    them are close to feasible: $\lambda_{\min}(G)$ lies between $-0.075$ and
    $-0.011$ over the 540 paired instances at $n=100$ and $n=500$. The degree of invalidity, which reorders
    the methods on the equity-derived matrices, is therefore not a controlled
    factor of the family, and the findings that depend on it rest on perturbed
    matrices with computed references.
    \Cref{sec:real} adds four matrices from the literature, from $n=94$ to
    $n=3250$, measured against a computed rather than an exact reference; for
    the two largest that reference is capped and reaches
    $\nrm{\nabla\theta}_2\approx10^{-12}$ rather than $10^{-13}$, and no
    wall-clock times were taken. Four matrices cannot separate dimension from
    the other ways in which they differ. The equity-based instances of
    \cref{sec:thesis} use a factor model of one correlation matrix as the
    exact solution; those instances are only mildly invalid and have three
    replicates per setting, while the perturbed set has five draws per
    $\sigma$ and a computed reference. The sets of \cref{sec:thesis} rest on
    one base correlation matrix and those of \cref{sec:us2105} on a second,
    so there are two panels in all, not a sample of them.
  \item \emph{Statistical uncertainty.} Aggregate medians at both dimensions
    carry cluster-bootstrap intervals (\cref{tab:fwd,tab:n500}). Each
    dimension rests on five paired seeds per rank, that is 15 seed clusters,
    so inferential statements remain modest. Cell-level
    orderings rest on five instances each, and within a cell the adjacent pair
    AGD-SDAJ-BH against AGD-SDAJ is usually unresolved, so the count of
    distinct orderings should not be read as a count of real regime changes.
    We report counts and intervals rather than significance tests, because the
    reuse of each drawn $U$ across all cells of its rank makes instance-level
    comparisons strongly dependent. The pooled medians at $n=100$ weight the
    three ranks equally, so the target at which a pooled median changes sign
    depends on the rank grid, and no correction for multiple comparisons is
    applied to the cell-level and target-level comparisons, which are
    exploratory.
  \item \emph{Single machine, five timings.} All timings come from one
    machine with single-threaded BLAS. \Cref{sec:evd-unit} repeats the
    matched-tolerance measurement five times in all (the session reported in
    \cref{tab:perevd} and four further sessions): a given solver's per-\EVD\
    cost varies by a factor of 3.8 to 4.9 across sessions, depending on the
    solver, at least twice the $\le\!1.50\times$ spread between solvers
    within one session, so per-\EVD\ ratios that differ by less than about 50\%
    should not be read as ordered, and the specific rank of the middle four
    or five solvers, by either \EVD\ count or wall-clock time, should not be
    treated as a fixed property of the methods (\cref{sec:evd-unit}). Only
    the leading position (Newton-SIN-BH cheapest overall) is robust across
    every session; Dykstra-APM is dearest overall in 8 of the 10 sessions
    across both dimensions, but not all. A swing of this size, uniform across
    every solver in a session (\cref{sec:evd-unit}), is consistent with a
    platform-level cause such as thermal throttling or a power-state change on
    a laptop-class part rather than with anything solver-specific; we did not
    pin the CPU power plan or record clock frequency during these sessions,
    so we cannot rule this out. This is itself an argument against reading
    wall-clock time alone as a stable property of a method on this kind of
    hardware, which is why we report \EVD\ counts as the primary unit of cost
    throughout and wall-clock time only as a secondary check.
  \item \emph{Parameters.} Each method in the main study ran at one parameter
    setting: the published values for Newton-SIN-BH, AGD-SDAJ, Dykstra-APM and
    Anderson-APM (history length $m=2$), and the specification of
    \cref{app:algorithms} for SBB-Dual, whose step-size clamp is $\eta=0.01$.
    As a check, we reran the 270 $n=100$ instances against AGD-SDAJ-BH at
    $\eta\in\{0.001,0.01,0.05,0.1,0.2\}$ and at
    $m\in\{1,2,3,5,10\}$ (\texttt{code/sensitivity\_eta\_m.jl}). SBB-Dual's
    median cost to $10^{-8}$ moves from 33.5 to 38.5 \EVDs\ over two orders of
    magnitude in $\eta$, and the count of instances on which it is cheaper
    than AGD-SDAJ-BH stays at 165--167 of 270 throughout, so the
    SBB-Dual/AGD-SDAJ-BH comparison is not sensitive to this parameter.
    Anderson-APM is more
    sensitive: its median cost falls from 36 at $m=1$ to 19--20 at $m=5$--$10$,
    and the count cheaper than AGD-SDAJ-BH rises from 210 to 257--261 of 270,
    non-monotonically past $m=5$. The published default $m=2$ (median 24,
    240/270) is therefore not the most favourable setting for Anderson-APM
    against AGD-SDAJ-BH at $n=100$ and $10^{-8}$; a larger $m$ would only
    improve that comparison at those conditions, not weaken it, though this
    was not checked against the full 11 conventions of \cref{sec:winner} or
    at other dimensions. The sensitivity of the rank-dependent orderings of
    \cref{sec:cells,sec:n500,sec:highrank,sec:thesis,sec:us2105} to these
    parameters was not checked.
  \item \emph{Feasibility propositions.} \Cref{prop:det} assumes uniform
    $\mu$, and the extension to mixed $\mu$ is open (\cref{app:family}).
    \Cref{prop:hd} is stated for $a$ only; no companion bound on $b$ is
    given, since the released family lies in the fixed-rank regime where
    \cref{prop:hd} does not apply.
  \item \emph{Scope of methods.} We benchmark six variants of five methods.
    Not included are the recent semismooth Newton method for general
    projection equations~\cite{armijo2025semismooth} and the factor-structured
    variant~\cite{borsdorf2010factor}. The main study runs Anderson
    acceleration only at the authors' default history length $m=2$; the
    \emph{Parameters} item above reports a sensitivity check covering
    $m\le10$, but at $n=100$ only.
  \item \emph{Attribution of the line search.} Newton-SIN-BH uses the
    published Borsdorf--Higham globalization, but not the rest of their
    algorithm (\cref{sec:solvers}); adapting their near-equality safeguard to
    AGD-SDAJ, which gives AGD-SDAJ-BH, is ours, not the original authors'.
    AGD-SDAJ-BH is reported beside unmodified AGD-SDAJ on every set where
    both were run (all except the 135 higher-rank instances), and
    Newton-SIN-BH beside the naive-Armijo control Newton-SIN in
    \cref{sec:protocol,sec:traps}.
  \item \emph{The author's own method.} SBB-Dual is from the author's
    companion manuscript~\cite{ibrahim2026sbb}. Two findings rest on it
    specifically, the bracketed positions of the rank crossover and its
    recurrence at a higher rank at $n=500$; the others hold for pairs that
    exclude it (\cref{sec:intro}).
  \item \emph{Unweighted problem.} Only the unweighted Frobenius problem is
    considered.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
An exact instance family with a prescribed solution structure makes it
possible to separate stopping-rule, reference, accounting and
primitive-work effects in NCM benchmarks. On 270 instances at $n=100$, these
effects changed cost ratios and the lower places of the ranking, but not the
cheapest method. Repeating the study on 270 instances at $n=500$ sharpened
that finding: Newton-SIN-BH remains the leading method, and, for SBB-Dual
against AGD-SDAJ-BH, the changes of
lower-place ordering with the accuracy target and with rank recur, but only
above $r=50$, a rank the original grid did not reach; for Dykstra-APM
against AGD-SDAJ-BH, the same rank-dependent reversal seen at $n=100$ has
not recurred by $r=150$ at $n=500$. Rankings
below the leading method are therefore not invariant across the benchmark
regimes tested; they depend materially on the benchmark
regime, including its
dimension, the rank of the solution and the size of the violation, and, for
the specific rank at which a reversal occurs, on which pair of methods is
compared. The one clear failure we found is a known finite-precision
line-search defect, which appears in two separately published methods, on
the constructed family and on real matrices. It is
removed in the semismooth Newton method by the published Borsdorf--Higham
modified line search, and in AGD-SDAJ by our adaptation of their near-equality
safeguard. We recommend
that NCM comparisons report forward error against a trusted solution at a
common tolerance, give rejected trials no accuracy credit, and report
primitive work and wall-clock time together.
