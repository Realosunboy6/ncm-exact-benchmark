\section{Ranking under different conventions}\label{sec:ranking}

\subsection{Solvers}\label{sec:solvers}
We compare six variants of five methods. All are implemented in Julia with
a common evaluation kernel, and every evaluation of $\theta$ or
$\nabla\theta$ costs exactly one \EVD. Unless stated otherwise, each variant
starts from $y_0=0$ (Dykstra-APM starts from $X_0=G$). The algorithms below
are written at the same level of detail and use the notation
$\bigl(\theta_k,g_k,X_k\bigr)\leftarrow\textsc{Eval}(y_k)$ for one \EVD\ of
$C(y_k)$, with $X_k=\Proj(C(y_k))$ and $g_k=\diag(X_k)-e$. The stopping
criterion in all algorithms is $\nrm{g_k}_2\le\tau$ (Dykstra-APM applies it to
the diagonal of its PSD iterate). Here $\tau=10^{-11}$ unless stated
otherwise.

\paragraph{Newton-SIN-BH} (\cref{alg:newton}) is the semismooth Newton
method of Qi and Sun~\cite{qisun2006quadratically}. The Newton system is solved inexactly by
conjugate gradients, using the block form of the generalized Jacobian
restricted to the positive-eigenvalue columns (with
$\Omega_{\beta\beta}=0$) and a $10^{-12}$ diagonal shift. The spectral
decomposition from the accepted trial is cached (\cref{sec:trap1}), and
globalization uses the Borsdorf--Higham modified line
search~\cite[Alg.~4.1, Step~6]{borsdorf2010preconditioned}. We adopt their
globalization only: their Algorithm~4.1 also solves the Newton system with
preconditioned \textsc{minres} rather than \textsc{cg}, which we do not
adopt, so Newton-SIN-BH is not a reimplementation of their
algorithm. \emph{Newton-SIN} is the same method with the
naive Armijo test in place of the Borsdorf--Higham globalization, and
without the cached factorization of \cref{sec:trap1} (so its \EVD\ counts
carry both traps at once); it appears only as a control in
\cref{sec:protocol,sec:traps}.

\paragraph{AGD-SDAJ} (\cref{alg:agd}) is Algorithm~4 of Huynh and
Hwang~\cite{huynh2025accelerated}, transcribed from the full text and checked against
the published counts (\cref{app:agd}). It uses $q=2$ inner quasi-Newton steps
with a diagonal secant preconditioner and the nonmonotone residual line search
of their equation~(6), followed by one accelerated gradient step with a
monotone Armijo line search. \emph{AGD-SDAJ-BH} is our adaptation of that
Armijo step, described in \cref{sec:trap2}.

\paragraph{SBB-Dual} (\cref{alg:sbb}) is a spectral (Barzilai--Borwein)
gradient method on~\eqref{eq:dual}. It uses the BB1 step, clamped to
$[\eta,2-\eta]$ with $\eta=0.01$, has no line search, performs one \EVD\ per
update, and finishes with a diagonal-rescaling step
$\hat X=D^{-1/2}XD^{-1/2}$, $D=\Diag(\diag X)$, as in~\cite{borsdorf2010preconditioned}.

\paragraph{Dykstra-APM} (\cref{alg:apm}) is Higham's alternating projections
with Dykstra's correction~\cite{higham2002nearest}. Each iteration uses one \EVD. The
method returns the PSD half-iterate.

\paragraph{Anderson-APM} (\cref{alg:anderson}) is Dykstra-APM with Anderson
acceleration, as given by Higham and Strabi\'{c}~\cite{higham2016anderson}. The
fixed-point map is one alternating-projections step on the pair $(Y,\Delta S)$,
and the acceleration solves a least-squares problem over the last $m$ differences
of its residuals, taken here at the authors' default $m=2$. It costs one \EVD\ per
iteration plus $O(mn^2)$ work, and like Dykstra-APM it is scored on the PSD
half-iterate. Our implementation transcribes their released code. Its iterates
agree with an independent implementation to $2.3\times10^{-12}$, and it
reproduces their published iteration counts where rounding at their stopping
tolerance does not decide them. Under the same eigensolver it matches both
large-matrix counts exactly and 61 of 64 small ones, with the rest one
iteration apart; on one ill-conditioned matrix the counts are decided by
rounding and differ by up to 56 iterations (\cref{app:anderson}).

\subsection{Design}
All six variants were run on the 270 instances of \cref{sec:family} at
$n=100$, to a common tolerance of $10^{-11}$, and a trajectory was recorded as
described in \cref{sec:traj}. Each solver ran under its own cap: 4000 \EVDs\
for the Newton and AGD variants, 20000 iterations for SBB-Dual and 2000 for the
two projection methods. Only unmodified AGD-SDAJ reached its cap at $n=100$
(\cref{sec:trap2}). We evaluated 11
conventions: the native exit; a common forward-error target
$\fro{X-\Xs}\le\varepsilon$ for
$\varepsilon\in\{10^{-2},10^{-4},10^{-6},10^{-8},10^{-10}\}$; and a common
dual residual $\nrm{\nabla\theta}_2\le\tau$ for the same five values. We also
compared accepted-only with all-trial accounting.
\Cref{sec:n500} repeats the study at $n=500$.

\subsection{Cost of the semismooth Newton method across conventions}\label{sec:winner}
The 11 conventions produce three distinct orderings, and Newton-SIN-BH is
cheapest in all three: it leads under every accuracy target, under both error
measures, under either accounting rule and at every solver's own exit, and it
is strictly cheapest on every one of the 270 instances at $10^{-8}$ and
$10^{-10}$. In median, Anderson-APM is second under 10 of the 11 conventions
and tied for second, with both AGD variants, under the loosest dual-residual
target. Per instance its hold is much weaker: it is strictly cheapest after
Newton-SIN-BH on 136 of the 270 instances at $10^{-8}$ and 139 at $10^{-10}$,
or on 157 and 142 counting ties. The conventions
change cost ratios and the order of the remaining places; they never change
which method is cheapest. The rest of this section is about those remaining
places, which is where the choice of test set decides the answer.

\subsection{Dependence of the ordering on the accuracy target}
\Cref{tab:fwd} and \cref{fig:crossover} show median cost to a common
target. Anderson-APM is second at every target, from 5 \EVDs\ at $10^{-2}$ to 32
at $10^{-10}$, where AGD-SDAJ-BH needs 63.5 and SBB-Dual 43.5. The places
below it move. In the pooled medians, both AGD variants are cheaper than
SBB-Dual at the two loosest targets ($10^{-2}$, $10^{-4}$), and SBB-Dual is
cheaper from $10^{-6}$ onward. The same reversal
occurs at the same point under the forward-error and the dual-residual
criteria, so it does not depend on which error measure is used. A paper that
reports at a loose tolerance would place AGD-SDAJ above SBB-Dual, and a paper
that reports at a tight tolerance would place them the other way. Both are
defensible one-number summaries of the same runs, although, as the paired
comparison below shows, the two methods are level at $10^{-4}$ and the change
of order is concentrated in one rank.

\paragraph{Reach and hold.}
A target counts as reached at the first accepted iterate from which the error
stays at or below the target for the rest of the run. The accelerated methods
are not monotone in forward error, so this differs from the first iterate at
which the error dips below the target; scoring the first dip alone would
understate their cost. Under this rule Newton-SIN-BH, AGD-SDAJ-BH, SBB-Dual
and Dykstra-APM reach every target on all 270 instances. Only unmodified
AGD-SDAJ misses some: 264/270 and 253/270 at forward targets $10^{-8}$ and
$10^{-10}$ (264 and 254 for the residual), which is consistent with its 18
budget-exhausting stalls (\cref{sec:trap2}). Its entries marked $^{*}$ are
medians over the instances it reached, and are therefore optimistic. The
second/third-place reversal is the same whether or not those entries are
included. A first-hit rule (the first accepted iterate with error at or below
the target, whether or not it holds) leaves four of the six solvers'
medians unchanged at every target, and moves Anderson-APM on 5 instances at
$10^{-2}$ (by 2 \EVDs\ each, median unaffected). It lowers the two AGD
variants' medians at the two tightest targets: AGD-SDAJ-BH from 45 to 43 at
$10^{-8}$ and from 63.5 to 62.5 at $10^{-10}$; AGD-SDAJ from 50 to 48 (also
reaching 266 instead of 264) and from 72 to 70 (257 instead of 253). The
disagreement is on 53 to 62 of 270 instances at those two targets (one
instance each at $10^{-6}$, not enough to move the median), 5 to 8 \EVDs\
apart there in median, at most 1542. Both shifts leave AGD-SDAJ-BH and
AGD-SDAJ well above SBB-Dual's 34 and 43.5, so \cref{tab:fwd}'s median
ordering does not depend on requiring sustained accuracy rather than a first
crossing, though the exact AGD medians do.
\paragraph{Uncertainty.}
The bootstrap intervals in \cref{tab:fwd} resample 15 clusters, so they
describe the variation between seeds and are narrow by construction; they do
not test the ordering. What does is the paired per-instance comparison, which
is much closer than the pooled medians suggest. The median paired difference
in \EVDs, SBB-Dual minus AGD-SDAJ-BH, with its cluster interval and the share
of the 270 instances on which SBB-Dual is cheaper, is
$+2$ $[1,2]$, 40.4\% at $10^{-2}$;
$+1.5$ $[-1,2]$, 48.5\% at $10^{-4}$;
$-4$ $[-5,-4]$, 54.1\% at $10^{-6}$;
$-11$ $[-13.5,-9]$, 61.9\% at $10^{-8}$; and
$-19$ $[-22,-17]$, 65.6\% at $10^{-10}$.
An instance-level sign test would treat the 270 comparisons as independent
although they form only 15 clusters, so it would overstate the evidence at
every target; we therefore do not report one. The cluster interval is a
better guide, but it is not free-standing evidence either: the bootstrap
resamples the five seed clusters within each rank separately and always
keeps the three ranks in equal proportion, so its sign is fixed whenever
every cluster within a rank already agrees on direction, as happens here.
Within a
rank the sign of the paired median does not change with the target at $r=5$
(AGD-SDAJ-BH cheaper, by 13.5 to 97.5 \EVDs) or at $r=50$ (SBB-Dual cheaper, by
1 to 26); it changes only at $r=20$, between $10^{-4}$ ($+1.5$) and $10^{-6}$
($-5$). From $10^{-6}$ on, then, the pooled interval excludes zero because
two of the three ranks agree with each other and outnumber the third under
equal weighting, not because 15 exchangeable clusters were resampled and
happened to concur: resampling all 15 together without stratifying by rank
spans zero at $10^{-6}$, $10^{-8}$ and $10^{-10}$ alike. The finding that
survives is the per-rank one stated above, not the pooled interval as an
independent confirmation of it. By the pooled standard the two methods are
level at $10^{-4}$ (win share 48.5\%, essentially even); AGD-SDAJ-BH is
cheaper at $10^{-2}$ by a modest median margin with substantial per-instance
overlap; and SBB-Dual is cheaper in the pooled median from $10^{-6}$, but on
only 54\%, 62\% and 66\% of the instances at $10^{-6}$, $10^{-8}$ and
$10^{-10}$. The dependence of the pooled comparison on the target is thus
entirely the rank dependence of \cref{sec:cells}, weighted equally across
the three ranks by the grid, and its position between $10^{-4}$ and
$10^{-6}$ is not a property of the methods alone. At $n=500$ (\cref{sec:n500}), SBB-Dual is cheaper than
AGD-SDAJ-BH on 0 of 270 instances at $10^{-2}$.

\subsection{Dependence of the ordering on the degeneracy parameters}\label{sec:cells}
Aggregate medians can hide dependence on the regime. At a fixed target
(forward error $10^{-8}$), the 54 $(r,m,\delta)$ cells produce 10
distinct orderings (\cref{tab:cells}). This description is exploratory: each
cell holds five instances, no correction for multiple comparisons is applied to
the cell-level orderings, and where two cell medians are equal the order
follows the fixed listing of the solvers. That affects seven cells in which
SBB-Dual and Anderson-APM tie exactly (six at $r=50$, one at $r=20$), so the
count of 10 depends on the tie-break. What is claimed below is the rank effect,
which is present in every one of the five seeds.

Newton-SIN-BH is cheapest in every cell. Below it, at $n=100$, the order is
governed almost entirely by the rank $r$ of the solution. AGD-SDAJ-BH is
cheaper than SBB-Dual in all 18 cells with $r=5$; SBB-Dual is cheaper in 16 of
the 18 cells with $r=20$ and in all 18 with $r=50$. Within a rank, the
near-zero multiplicity $m$ and the separation $\delta$ rarely change
this pair's order. Both AGD variants fall below Dykstra-APM to last place in
14 cells: all 12 cells with $r=50$ and $m\in\{5,20\}$, and the two cells with
$r=20$, $m=20$ and $\delta\in\{10^{-8},10^{-10}\}$. This block is determined
by rank and multiplicity; the separation plays no visible role in it. The
instance family was built to vary these quantities one at a time. On a
collection of real matrices with uncontrolled spectra, the ordering could not
be associated with rank in this way.

\paragraph{A crossover between a published method and our adaptation of one.}
The reversal is not confined to SBB-Dual. Dykstra-APM, a published method,
and AGD-SDAJ-BH, our Borsdorf--Higham adaptation of a published method
(\cref{sec:trap2}), exchange places over the same rank range. At $r=5$
AGD-SDAJ-BH is cheaper on all 90 instances at $10^{-8}$ (median difference
$+223.5$ \EVDs) and at $r=20$ on 80 of 90, with one tie; at $r=50$ Dykstra-APM
is cheaper on 61 of 90 (median $-3.5$, cluster-bootstrap 95\% interval
$[-6,-2]$) and on 75 of 90 at $10^{-10}$ ($-8.5$, $[-12,-5]$); with only 5
seed clusters at this rank, these within-rank intervals are close to the
range of the 5 seed medians, so the evidence for the direction is really
the unanimous sign across those 5, not an independent interval on top of
it. All five seeds
agree on the sign at $r=5$ and at $r=50$, though 5-of-5 agreement alone
gives a two-sided sign-test $p=0.0625$, above the conventional 0.05
threshold for either rank taken alone; it is the same sign recurring at
$r=20$ and, oppositely, at $r=5$ that gives the pooled 10-of-10 pattern its
strength ($p\approx0.002$). The direction at $r=50$ is resolved
but the margin is small, about an eighth of AGD-SDAJ-BH's median cost of 28
\EVDs, and 26 of the 90 instances go the other way. The reversal is therefore
reproducible without SBB-Dual, on a pair neither of which is the author's own
new method. It does not occur by the
same rank at higher dimension: at $n=500$
AGD-SDAJ-BH is still ahead at $r=150$ (Dykstra-APM cheaper on 13 of 45 at
$10^{-10}$, \cref{sec:highrank}). On the equity panel at $n=2105$ the
corresponding pair is Anderson-APM and AGD-SDAJ-BH, whose order is not
monotone in rank: Anderson-APM is cheaper at $r=50$ and $r=400$ and
AGD-SDAJ-BH at $r=100$ and $r=200$ (\cref{sec:us2105}).

The location of the SBB-Dual/AGD-SDAJ-BH crossover is nevertheless specific to $n=100$. At
$n=500$ (\cref{sec:n500,sec:highrank}) SBB-Dual is behind at every rank up to
$r=50$ and ahead from $r=75$: both the direction of the effect and the
crossover survive, but the crossover moves to a higher rank.

That rankings depend on the required accuracy is expected in
principle~\cite{more2009benchmarking,hansen2021coco}. What the controlled
family adds is that the variation is organized by a single spectral quantity
which the construction sets directly.

Each cell holds five instances, so the count of distinct orderings overstates
how much genuinely varies. Resampling the paired seeds of each cell ($B=2000$)
leaves the adjacent pair AGD-SDAJ-BH against AGD-SDAJ unresolved in most cells,
as expected of two methods differing only in a safeguard that rarely activates
at $n=100$; reading unresolved pairs as ties collapses much of
\cref{tab:cells}. What survives is the placement of SBB-Dual relative to the
AGD pair, resolved in the great majority of cells.
\Cref{fig:heatmap} shows the same comparison cell by cell as a ratio of median
costs, which separates the three controlled quantities: rank sets the level,
the near-zero multiplicity modulates it within a rank, and the separation has
almost no effect.

\emph{Confirmed.} Newton-SIN-BH is again cheapest at every target, at 5 \EVDs.
The stall of unmodified AGD-SDAJ recurs (31 of 270 runs exhaust the \EVD\

\emph{Not reproduced for $r\le50$.} The change of order between SBB-Dual and
the AGD variants seen in \cref{tab:fwd} is absent: at $n=500$ AGD-SDAJ-BH is
ahead of SBB-Dual at all five targets. Only Anderson-APM moves, from fourth
at $10^{-2}$ to second from $10^{-4}$ on (\cref{tab:n500}). The paired
per-instance comparison is one-sided. SBB-Dual is cheaper on 0 of 270
instances at $10^{-2}$, 3 at $10^{-4}$ and 15 at $10^{-8}$. We report
these counts and the cluster-bootstrap intervals of \cref{tab:n500} rather
than an instance-level significance test: the design reuses each drawn $U$
across all 18 $(m,\delta)$ cells of its rank, so the 270 comparisons form 15
seed clusters, and a test treating them as independent would overstate
the evidence by a wide margin. The rank effect of \cref{sec:cells} keeps its
direction but no longer produces a crossover: the median paired difference at
$10^{-8}$ falls from $+669.5$ \EVDs\ at $r=5$ to $+129$ at $r=20$ and $+13$ at
$r=50$, where SBB-Dual is cheaper on 17\% of instances. Cell by cell, SBB-Dual
is ahead of AGD-SDAJ-BH in 0 of the 54 cells with a resolved bootstrap
interval (47 resolved in favour of AGD-SDAJ-BH, 7 unresolved); with 5 seed
clusters per cell, a resolved interval here is again close to unanimous
agreement across those 5, not independent confirmation on top of it. SBB-Dual is thus
behind at every rank tested at $n=500$, while it led at $r\ge20$ at $n=100$.
The shrinking gap, however, points to a crossover beyond the grid, which
\cref{sec:highrank} confirms.

This is not an artefact of the caps. SBB-Dual converged on all 270 instances
(worst case 1075 \EVDs\ against its 1200 cap) and reached every target except
$10^{-10}$ on nine instances. $^{\ddagger}$Dykstra-APM, by contrast, \emph{is}
censored: 90 of 270 runs stop at the 800-iteration cap, and from $10^{-6}$
down its medians cover only 180 instances. Its position at $n=500$ should be
read as censored rather than measured.

The scaling is the cleaner way to state the difference. Between $n=100$ and
$n=500$ the median cost to $10^{-8}$ rises by a factor of 6.1 for SBB-Dual
(34 to 207.5 \EVDs) and by 1.8 for AGD-SDAJ-BH (45 to 79), while Newton-SIN-BH
is unchanged at 5. The order below first place therefore reflects the test set,
including its dimension, rather than a fixed property of the methods.

\subsection{Instances built from equity returns}\label{sec:thesis}
Two further test sets start from one real correlation matrix: the sample
correlation matrix $C$ of daily log returns, computed from split-adjusted
closing prices, of 550 US-traded equities over 1397 trading days (January 2020
to July 2025), with no missing values. The panel holds 50 names under each of
the eleven sector labels of its source files and includes some ADRs and one
closed-end fund. $C$ is a valid correlation matrix, with
$\lambda_{\min}(C)=0.019$. It serves here only as a realistic input, not as an
estimate of market structure; one series (CHRD) contains a corporate-action
splice, which does not affect that use.

\paragraph{KKT instances seeded by the data.}
Because $C$ has full rank it cannot itself be the solution of a nontrivial
instance of \cref{sec:family}, whose construction needs a kernel. Its rank-$r$
factor model can: with $C\approx V_r\Lambda_rV_r^{\mathsf T}$, set
$U=V_r\Lambda_r^{1/2}$, scale its rows to unit norm and take $\Xs=UU^{\mathsf T}$.
The construction of \cref{sec:family} then gives $G$ with $\Xs$ as its
\emph{exact} nearest correlation matrix, whose eigenvectors come from the
market data rather than from a random $U$. Replicates differ by a Haar-random
rotation of the kernel basis. Two settings differ from the synthetic family,
both forced by screening: at $r=5$ the factor model misses 95\% of the
off-diagonal correlation and drives some $|\Xs_{ij}|$ to $0.99995$, so every
$r=5$ instance fails $|G_{ij}|\le1$, and with $\mu_{\mathrm{bulk}}=0.1$ every
rank fails it. We therefore use $r\in\{20,50,100\}$ (off-diagonal relative fit
0.59, 0.39, 0.24) and $\mu_{\mathrm{bulk}}=0.01$, with
$\delta\in\{10^{-5},10^{-9},0\}$ so that $\sep=\delta/\mu_{\mathrm{bulk}}$ stays
on the synthetic grid, $m\in\{1,5,20\}$ and three rotations: 81 instances. All
pass screening, with worst exactness $1.1\times10^{-12}$. They are only mildly
invalid, $\lambda_{\min}(G)\in[-4.6,-1.2]\times10^{-3}$.

\subsection{An equity panel at $n=2105$}\label{sec:us2105}

853 of its eigenvalues are zero to working precision, and $C$ sits exactly on
the boundary of the feasible set.

\subsection{Four matrices from the literature}\label{sec:real}
The family of \cref{sec:family} is synthetic. To check that the regime
dependence above is not an artefact of the construction, we ran the same
protocol on four invalid correlation matrices from the literature: the
Rocky Mountain carbon-storage matrix ($n=94$), \texttt{cor1399} ($n=1399$),
\texttt{cor3120} ($n=3120$) and \texttt{bccd16} ($n=3250$), all from the
Anymatrix \texttt{CORRINV} collection~\cite{higham2022anymatrix} used
by~\cite{huynh2025accelerated}.

No exact solution is available here, which is the situation the rest of this
paper avoids. Forward error is measured against a reference computed by
semismooth Newton driven towards $10^{-13}$; for \texttt{cor1399} that
reference solve itself stopped at its iteration limit after 1770 \EVDs\ with
$\nrm{\nabla\theta}_2=4.59\times10^{-13}$. At $n\approx3000$ an absolute
tolerance of $10^{-13}$ is not attainable in double precision, so for
\texttt{cor3120} and \texttt{bccd16} the reference solve is capped at 30
\EVDs\ and its achieved residual is reported instead:
$\nrm{\nabla\theta}_2=1.25\times10^{-12}$ and $5.03\times10^{-13}$, after
2506\,s and 444\,s in the recorded check run
(\texttt{results/validation/reference\_check.log}). A second reference for
each, differently initialized (SBB-Dual driven
to its floor, then the same Newton polish that produced the first reference)
rather than by an independent algorithm, agrees with the first to
$\fro{X_A-X_B}=1.3\times10^{-11}$ and $8.1\times10^{-12}$, so forward errors on
these matrices are resolved well below the targets used, and are insensitive
to the starting point; the residual floor
near $10^{-12}$ is reached within four to six Newton iterations. We therefore treat
these runs as a check on external
validity, not as measurements of the same quality as \cref{tab:fwd}. Every
solver is run to a common tolerance of $10^{-9}$, capped at 500 \EVDs\ on the
two smaller matrices and at 300 iterations on the two larger ones, and costs
are read at the forward-error target $10^{-8}$.

\paragraph{The line-search stall occurs on real data.}
On the Rocky Mountain matrix the naive-Armijo Newton control exhausts its
budget at a forward error of $1.03\times10^{-8}$, while the same solver with
the Borsdorf--Higham line search converges in 4 \EVDs\ to
$1.49\times10^{-13}$. On \texttt{cor1399} the naive control converges normally
(13 \EVDs\ against 7). On \texttt{bccd16} the same defect appears in
unmodified AGD-SDAJ. Both AGD variants follow an identical path to a forward
error of $3.93\times10^{-5}$ at the fifth \EVD. From there AGD-SDAJ-BH, whose
safeguard accepts the step, converges in four more \EVDs\ to
$8.9\times10^{-10}$; unmodified AGD-SDAJ rejects trial after trial in its
monotone line search, with the error unchanged in its first eight digits, until
it exhausts the budget at 323 \EVDs. The defect of \cref{sec:trap2} is thus not
an artefact of the constructed family, and it affects both published methods on
real data, but neither is it universal: whether it appears depends on the
matrix. Unmodified AGD-SDAJ also stalls on 10 of the 81 data-seeded and 1 of
the 20 perturbed instances of \cref{sec:thesis}, and on \texttt{mmb13},
\texttt{tyda99r2} and \texttt{bhwi01} (\cref{app:agd}).

\paragraph{The cost of a reference.}
At $n\approx3000$ the reference solve alone took 7 to 42 minutes in the
recorded check run, and a first
attempt at \texttt{bccd16} with an uncapped absolute tolerance ran for four
hours without finishing. A reference accurate enough to measure forward error
can cost more than the solves being measured. That is a practical argument for
the synthetic family, whose solution is available in closed form at any size.

\subsection{Accounting convention}\label{sec:acct-results}
Under all-trial accounting, every evaluated point counts toward reaching and
holding a target, including rejected line-search trials. A rejected trial can
then be credited with reaching a target, but one that lies above the target
also breaks a hold. On this family the second effect dominates slightly.
Median costs change only for the two AGD variants, and all-trial accounting
is marginally the more expensive: for AGD-SDAJ-BH the median rises by 1.0, 1.5
and 2.0 \EVDs\ at $\varepsilon=10^{-4},10^{-8},10^{-10}$, and for unmodified
AGD-SDAJ by 1.0, 2.0 and 2.0.
Newton-SIN-BH, SBB-Dual and Dykstra-APM are unaffected, because they have few
or no rejected trials here. The effect is negligible on this family and never
changes an ordering. For methods with many rejected trials it need not be,
and accepted-only accounting is the rule that cannot credit a point the method
itself rejected.

Two further accounting choices deserve a check, because each could favour a
particular method. First, cost is scored on the raw projection
$\Proj(G+\Diag(y))$, while SBB-Dual applies a Borsdorf--Higham rescaling before
returning its iterate (\cref{sec:solvers}), so it is scored on a quantity it
does not return. Both columns are recorded. Rescoring every study on the
rescaled iterate leaves the ordering unchanged at 19 of the 20
(dataset, target) pairs, and moves SBB-Dual's median at $n=500$ and
$\varepsilon=10^{-8}$ from 207.5 to 188 \EVDs: the reported numbers are the
conservative choice for SBB-Dual on cost, in the 19 pairs where the ordering
is unaffected. The exception cuts the other way: on the data-seeded set at
$10^{-10}$, rescoring lowers SBB-Dual's reach count from 81 to 45 instances,
so the raw-projection score used throughout this paper is actually the
\emph{favourable} choice for SBB-Dual there (it lets more of its runs count
as reaching the target), and the apparent change of order at that one
(dataset, target) pair is a comparison over an easier subset rather than a
genuine reversal. Second, the AGD line searches return
their last trial if a backtracking limit of 60 is reached, and the method then
moves to a point the trajectory records as rejected. This never occurs in the
reported runs: no line search in any study exceeded 60 trials, and every
AGD stall recorded here is a work-budget exhaustion, not a backtracking
failure.

The iteration caps are not uniform across methods, and no comparison in this
paper relies on a capped run. At $n=500$ the caps are 1200 iterations for
SBB-Dual, 800 for Dykstra-APM and a 4000-\EVD\ budget for the AGD variants,
chosen so that each method's own exit is reached on most instances within a
fixed total runtime. SBB-Dual converged on all 270 instances (worst case
1075), so its numbers are uncensored, and the AGD-SDAJ medians are taken over
the instances that reached each target, which understates the cost of the
unmodified variant and therefore understates the benefit of the safeguard.

Dykstra-APM is the one method whose cap binds, on 90 of 270 instances, and it
also had the smallest budget, so we re-ran those 90 at SBB-Dual's cap of 1200
iterations. \emph{All 90 reached the larger cap as well.} At 1200 iterations
they end at a median forward error of about $10^{-6}$: 71 of the 90 reach
$10^{-6}$ (median 1099 \EVDs, against 152 for SBB-Dual and 53.5 for
AGD-SDAJ-BH at the same target), 6 reach $10^{-8}$, and none reaches
$10^{-10}$. The censoring is therefore a property of the method on these
instances and not of the budget it was given; a cap generous enough to
uncensor Dykstra-APM would have to be an order of magnitude larger than any
other method needs. We report its position as censored throughout.



\subsection{What each solver returns}\label{sec:returned}
Methods also differ in what they return (\cref{tab:feas}). All six return
matrices with $\lambda_{\min}$ at roundoff level, so none is meaningfully more
positive semidefinite than another. The diagonal is a different matter.
SBB-Dual returns an exactly unit diagonal because of its rescaling epilogue,
whereas every other variant returns a diagonal that is accurate only to its
convergence tolerance. The worst-case diagonal error of unmodified AGD-SDAJ
is $4.01\times10^{-8}$, about 3.6 orders of magnitude larger than that of any
other variant, which is a direct consequence of the stalls in
\cref{sec:trap2}. The rescaling epilogue has a cost of its own: on a
nearly feasible input at loose tolerance, it can increase $\fro{X-\Xs}$.
Borsdorf and Higham bound that increase by
$\mathrm{tol}\,\fro{X}/(1-\mathrm{tol})$~\cite[Lem.~3.2]{borsdorf2010preconditioned},
which is small whenever the tolerance is tight but need not be at a loose
one.
A benchmark that reports only cost hides these differences. A user who needs
an exactly unit diagonal and a user who needs minimal distance may not be
best served by the same method.
