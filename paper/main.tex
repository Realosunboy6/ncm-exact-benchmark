\begin{abstract}
Nearest correlation matrix (NCM) solvers are usually compared on matrices whose
solutions are unknown, so the accuracy each delivers goes unmeasured. We
specialize the known technique of generating test problems from prescribed
optimality conditions to the NCM problem, obtaining instances with closed-form
primal and dual solutions and independently controlled rank, multiplicity and
eigenvalue separation. Measuring forward error against them
changes what a comparison reports. The semismooth Newton method with the
Borsdorf--Higham line search is cheapest under all 11 conventions examined, at
a cost flat in dimension: five eigendecompositions at
$n=100$ and again at $n=2105$. Second under 10 of them is Anderson-accelerated
alternating projections, a method absent from recent NCM comparisons. The
ordering below is a property of the test set, changing with the accuracy target
and above all with the solution rank: two published methods exchange places
between $r=20$ and $r=50$ at $n=100$, and that rank rises with dimension. Two further results are practical. The dimension-scaled rule
$\nrm{\nabla\theta}_2\le10^{-7}n$ permits 340 times less accuracy at $n=500$ than at
$n=100$, so a larger problem can appear easier. And a known finite-precision
failure of objective-value Armijo tests recurs in a second published method,
where a safeguard from the NCM literature removes it.
\end{abstract}
