\begin{abstract}
Nearest correlation matrix (NCM) solvers are usually compared on matrices whose
solutions are unknown, so the accuracy each delivers goes unmeasured. We
specialize the known technique of generating test problems from prescribed
optimality conditions to the NCM problem, obtaining instances with closed-form
primal and dual solutions and prescribed rank, multiplicity and
eigenvalue separation. The semismooth Newton method with the
Borsdorf--Higham line search is cheapest under all 11 conventions examined,
with a median eigendecomposition count that stays at five across the tested
exact-solution families, from $n=100$ to $n=2105$. Second at $n=100$, under ten of the eleven
conventions and tied under the other, is Anderson-accelerated alternating
projections. The ordering of the remaining methods is a property
of the test set, changing above all with the solution rank: two published
methods exchange places between $r=20$ and $r=50$ at $n=100$. Under the
dimension-scaled rule
$\nrm{\nabla\theta}_2\le10^{-7}n$, first-order methods return about ten times
less accuracy at $n=500$ than at $n=100$, so a larger problem can appear
easier. A known finite-precision failure of objective-value Armijo tests
recurs in a second published method, where a safeguard from the NCM literature
removes it.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Keywords.} nearest correlation matrix; semidefinite programming;
