\begin{abstract}
We specialize the known technique of generating test problems from prescribed
optimality conditions to the nearest correlation matrix (NCM) problem,
obtaining instances with exactly known primal and dual solutions whose
solution rank, near-zero multiplicity and eigenvalue separation are set
independently. Using a matched-accuracy protocol measured against the exact
solution, we show that conclusions drawn from NCM solver comparisons do not
transfer across benchmark regimes. At $n=100$ the order of SBB-Dual and
AGD-SDAJ reverses with the accuracy target and with solution rank, crossing
between $r=5$ and $r=20$. At $n=500$ both patterns persist, but the crossover
moves to between $r=50$ and $r=75$, so a study confined to $r\le50$ would
conclude that it had vanished. On matrices built from the returns of 550
equities the order also changes with the degree of invalidity, and four
matrices from the literature, from $n=94$ to $n=3250$, are consistent with
these regimes. The semismooth Newton method with the Borsdorf--Higham line
search is the cheapest method under every convention and on every test set.
The dimension-scaled stopping rule $\nrm{\nabla\theta}_2\le10^{-7}n$ permits
roughly 340 times less accuracy at $n=500$ than at $n=100$, so a larger
problem can appear easier. Finally, a known finite-precision failure of
objective-value Armijo tests recurs in a second published method, AGD-SDAJ.
It does not affect the published results, but it stalls the method on real
and data-derived matrices, including one at $n=3250$ that the safeguarded
variant solves in 9 \EVDs. It is removed in the Newton method by the
published Borsdorf--Higham line search, and in AGD-SDAJ by our adaptation of
their safeguard.
\end{abstract}
