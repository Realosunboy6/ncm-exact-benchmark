\begin{abstract}
We specialize the known technique of generating test problems from prescribed
optimality conditions to the nearest correlation matrix (NCM) problem,
obtaining instances with exactly known primal and dual solutions whose
solution rank, near-zero multiplicity and eigenvalue separation are set
independently. Using a matched-accuracy protocol measured against the exact
solution, we show that conclusions drawn from NCM solver comparisons do not
transfer across benchmark regimes. On 270 instances at $n=100$, the order of
SBB-Dual and AGD-SDAJ reverses with the accuracy target and is organized by
solution rank. On 162 instances at $n=500$ neither pattern survives:
AGD-SDAJ-BH is cheaper at every target and every rank. Two matrices from the
literature, at $n=94$ and $n=1399$, show the same dependence on dimension. The
semismooth Newton method with the Borsdorf--Higham line search is the
cheapest method under every convention and dimension tested. The
dimension-scaled stopping rule $\nrm{\nabla\theta}_2\le10^{-7}n$ permits
roughly 340 times less accuracy at $n=500$ than at $n=100$, so a larger problem
can appear easier. Finally, a known finite-precision failure of objective-value
Armijo tests recurs in a second published method, AGD-SDAJ, where it does not
affect the published results, and occurs on a real matrix, where the naive test
costs at least 75 times the work for five fewer correct digits. It is removed
in the Newton method by the published Borsdorf--Higham line search, and in
AGD-SDAJ by our adaptation of their safeguard.
\end{abstract}
