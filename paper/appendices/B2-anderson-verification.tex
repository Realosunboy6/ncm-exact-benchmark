\section{Anderson-APM verification record}\label{app:anderson}

\paragraph{Source and implementation.}
Anderson-APM is a transcription of \texttt{nearcorr\_aa.m} from the code
released with Higham and Strabi\'{c}~\cite{higham2016anderson}
(\texttt{github.com/higham/anderson-accel-ncm}). It keeps the order of the
convergence test and the update, the bookkeeping of the stored history, the
modified Gram--Schmidt update of the QR factors, MATLAB's \texttt{qrdelete} for
the oldest column, the optional column dropping (off by default) and the
returned matrix. Only the default least-squares option, QR with updating, is
transcribed; the paper's experiments use it.

\paragraph{What was compared.}
Their Tables 1, 3, 4 and 7 report iteration counts on matrices that are
public: four small matrices at history lengths $m=1,\dots,6$ and three
eigenvalue floors $\delta$ (72 counts), two fixed-element problems at
$m=1,\dots,5$ (10 counts), and \texttt{cor1399} and \texttt{cor3120} at $m=2$.
The RiskMetrics matrices and the $n=90$ matrix of their Table~4 are not public.
Their stopping test is $\fro{Y-X}/\fro{Y}<n\,\mathrm{eps}$.

\paragraph{Why counts differ.}
Under the default eigensolver the two large counts are 118 and 163, against
published values of 124 and 174. Two checks separate an error of transcription
from rounding at the stopping tolerance. First, an independent implementation
that solves each least-squares problem from scratch, without updating, gives
the same iterates as ours to within $2.3\times10^{-12}$ in relative Frobenius
norm until the stopping quantity reaches $10^{-10}$ (below which rounding
dominates the comparison), and the same iteration
count at the tolerances $10^{-8}$ and $10^{-10}$ in all 82 small cases. Second,
changing only the eigensolver moves the counts at $n\,\mathrm{eps}$ onto the
published ones: under \texttt{syevd} both large counts are reproduced exactly,
and 61 of the 64 small ones outside \texttt{mmb13} (\cref{tab:anderson-verify}); the three that remain are
one iteration apart. On \texttt{cor1399}, under the default eigensolver, the
stopping quantity spends the four iterations before it crosses between one
and four times the tolerance, so the step at which it first falls below is
decided by the last bits of each eigendecomposition.

\paragraph{The matrix \texttt{mmb13}.}
This is the order-6 matrix on which unaccelerated alternating projections needs
801 iterations. Near convergence the stopping quantity stays within a factor of
three of the tolerance for tens to hundreds of iterations, so the count at which
it first falls below is decided by rounding, and the gaps are large, up to 56
iterations: at $\delta=0$ and $m=4$ the published count is 126 and ours 70. The two
independent implementations disagree with each other by similar amounts on this
matrix while agreeing on its iterates to $10^{-12}$, and the published counts
are themselves not monotone in $m$ (117 at $m=3$, 126 at $m=4$). We regard the
counts on this matrix as uninformative about correctness either way.

\paragraph{What is claimed.}
We claim agreement of the iteration, not of every published count: our iterates
agree with an independent implementation, and our counts agree with the
published ones wherever rounding at the stopping tolerance does not decide
them. No result in this paper depends on those counts. The ranking study stops
at $\nrm{\diag(X)-e}_2\le10^{-11}$ and scores the recorded trajectory, and it
uses the default eigensolver, as every other solver does. The comparison
scripts and their logs are listed in \cref{app:artifact}.
